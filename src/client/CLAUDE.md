# src/client — browser

Applies together with the root `CLAUDE.md`. **Web on PC first** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): adapting for mobile is its own stage later; until then mobile only needs to open without errors (do not delete the media queries or touch input).

- **Content is data in `src/content`** (Nov 14): the story party and the recruit names (`party.ts`), the dungeon crawl tables (`crawl/rooms.ts`) and the walkable town (`crawl/town.ts`). The party's types and rules stay here (`group.ts`).
- **Workflow:** one developer (Fernando) commits straight to `master`, no PRs; there is a single live environment, https://dungeon-tactics.fernandorffdev.com.
- **Who owns what:** `main.ts` (`Game`) wires everything and owns the turn loop and the turn screen; `game/` holds what opens around it: the URL boot (`boot.ts`, `selectBattle`), the title and its windows (`title.ts`), Options (`options.ts`), the end-of-battle windows (`end-screens.ts`), the walkable town (`town.ts`, its rules in `town-map.ts`, pure and tested) and the town menu, the gate and the expedition (`crawl.ts`); `command.ts` says what the menu shows and what comes next (pure, tested); `interaction.ts` decides what the chosen tile does (pure, tested); `scene.ts` (`BattleScene`) only draws, composing the parts in `scene/` (`Board`, `Marks`, `Units`, `CameraRig`, `TileSpace`, `Timeline`), each built from what it needs; `hud.ts` (`BattleHud`) only shows and passes clicks along; `touch-input.ts` (`TouchInput`) only translates gestures and mouse; `gamepad.ts` translates the gamepad into keyboard actions (pure, tested). A class holding a resource (WebGL, listeners) has `dispose()`.
- **Interface in the FFT battle-screen style** (Fernando's decision, Nov 12: "discard the current interfaces and recreate the interface the same way as the original final fantasy tactics"). **The interface follows the design system (`docs/game-design/design-system.md`) as the source of truth**; the list below says what is implemented:
  - **Turn screens** (`Screen` in `command.ts`): command menu → abilities submenu → map (choose the tile) → "Execute?" → menu. Waiting leads to the direction choice on maps with direction.
  - **`BattleHud` pieces:**

    - painted faces (Nov 15, Fernando's request): the unit panels and the turn list show the dialogue box's sheet (`faceSheet` in `talk.ts`) instead of the portrait, with an expression from the events `Game.animate` plays: pure rules in `battle-face.ts` (sheet by class, `faceCues`, `expressionAt`, tested), moods kept by `Game` on the battle clock (`BattleScene.now`), frames moved every frame by `BattleHud.showFaces` without a redraw; the end-of-battle board shows a hero card per hero who fought, the fallen included (`resultBoard` in `game/end-screens.ts`, heroes from `Game.endHeroes`: the roster, since the state drops the fallen), with a still face (`stillFace` in `hud.ts`) by `endExpression`; outside the battle the Party rows, the Sheet head and the Tavern's recruit buttons show the class's neutral still face (`town-ui.ts`). Units without a sheet keep `portrait()`;
    - top middle: the help line and, below it, the ability name banner;
    - beside the acting character (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "beside the character like in FFT"): Move · Act · Wait · Status menu, submenu (since Nov 15 at the lab kit's size: a head with the menu name, 60 px rows with the 32 px ability icon or a glass orb before the name, Act marked as picked while the submenu is open) and the "Execute?" window with hit chance, effect and life before → after. `BattleScene.screenOf` gives the character on screen, `placeBeside` (`hud.ts`, pure and tested) picks the side (right; with no room, left) and slides in height so as not to cover the turn list or the "Execute?" target (with no room clear of both, "Execute?" keeps only the turn list clear); `Game.placeMenu` repositions every frame (only left/top, without redoing the DOM), because the camera slides and rotates with the menu open;
    - moved and did not act: the Move row becomes "Undo move" and Esc (B, right button) on the menu undoes the move, for free, like Tactics Ogre (Nov 13, Fernando's request; `canUndoMove` in `command.ts`). After acting, only Z undoes;
    - charges (Nov 15, Fernando): confirming a charge aimed at a unit adds a lock step in the same "Execute?" window (On the character / On the tile, starting on the ability's default lock; arrows switch, No goes back to "Execute?"; `needsLock` in `command.ts`), and its note says when it goes off and the movement left after starting it. Every charging unit, enemies included, keeps a line to what it is locked on, and on the charging unit's Move screen the line is traced again from the tile under the cursor, with the help line naming who it would hit (`charge-lines.ts`, pure and tested, from the rule that fires the charge; drawn by `scene/charge-lines.ts`, over everything): past the point where the shot stops the line goes red with an X, orange on the caster's own side (friendly fire, half). Counters the same way (Nov 15, Fernando; redefined the same day: only in the turn of the strike, on walking out of the struck unit's reach or, for a shooter struck up close, out of its contact): aiming, each target that could strike the actor where it stands, or a shooter it stands too close to (as the arrow), shows it (`countersOwed`: a ranged basic attack as its shot's line back to the attacker, a melee one as a violet arrow over its head pointing at it), named in "Execute?"; on the Move screen after a strike, the stops that draw a counter (out of a reach, or out of a shooter's contact and in its range, its line then ending at the stop) get a red frame (`counter` in `highlights`), and on such a stop the counter's line or arrow (`countersFrom`, from `pendingCounters`), the move help and a note on the cursor with the Strike icon and the damage at half (`counterDamage`, `BattleScene.setCursorNote`);
    - bottom: panel of the acting unit (left) and of the unit under the cursor (right), with portrait and HP, MP and CT bars (since Nov 15 at the lab kit's size, `--slot-w`; below 1600 px wide the portrait and the command menu shrink, which at the kit's size covered the unit at 1280). The target panel is mirrored (portrait on the right). When aiming (Act map and "Execute?"), the strike preview comes out (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "the attack preview interface should be like this one from Tactics Ogre (Reborn)"; our own design; since Nov 15 the lab kit's two-colour strip): beside the acting unit's panel, in the target panel's place, one strip per target (`strikes`, up to 3, the rest in "+N targets"), the action half on the panel blue (ability icon and name, the big damage −12–15 PTS or +7–10 in green on a heal, ⊕ hit chance) and the target half on its side's colour (wine for an enemy) with name and side, the life bar with the part that goes away in stripes and the `strikeInfo` line (↶ from behind / ⇥ from the side, push and spikes, "life 25 → 13–16", ALLY!). The calculation is one (`forecast` in `hud.ts`) for the strips and for "Execute?". The strips go into `sizes.bottom` (so "Execute?" does not land on top); one or two stay within the acting panel's height, three rise about 100 px above it at 1920; only the acting unit's panel counts in the height the scene discounts (`insets`), otherwise the map jumped when aiming;
    - top right corner: menu button (3 bars, on the panel blue) that opens the list Undo, rotate, view the map, pixel, Fast battle and Options, each row with its key (Nov 13, Fernando's request: the row of 7 stone buttons was "crude and inconsistent"); the performance counter right below.

Inside them, light text in `--ink`/`--ink-muted`, highlight and link in `--focus` (yellow), since Nov 15 the title on the head band, a bare ✕ and the lab kit's dark blue buttons with the steel trim (the primary in a brighter blue); lists of places (title, Quick battle, Town) as menu rows on the real focus; the end-of-battle board with a card per hero (`game/end-screens.ts`); every text colour passes 4.5:1. The pieces are in the design system.
  - **Design system** (Nov 13, Fernando's request): tokens, pieces with their states, input, measured contrast and debts in `docs/game-design/design-system.md`. Change a token or a piece, change the document in the same commit. Focus outside the panels takes `--focus-halo`; a disabled button says why in its text.
  - **New HUD piece:** `.panel` (blue gradient) with `.item` (menu row, `.focus` marks the focus), `gauge()` (bar with label and number) and `unitPanel()` in `hud.ts`. Colors and sizes only through the `:root` variables.
- **Input:** keyboard, mouse and gamepad move the same cursor and go through the same `Game.runKey` (in the walkable town, `Town.run`).
  - Keyboard: `keyboard.ts` (WASD and arrows go up and down the menu and move the cursor on the map; Enter confirms; Esc goes back; P Wait; C Status; F3 counter; Z undo; PgUp and PgDn scroll the turn list).
  - Mouse: hovering chooses, click confirms, right button goes back.
  - Gamepad (Gamepad API, standard Xbox and PlayStation mapping): d-pad and analog stick move; A or × confirms; B or ○ goes back; X or □ Status; shoulders rotate the camera; triggers zoom; right stick up and down scrolls the turn list; Back undoes; Start opens the menu. With a dialog open, the gamepad moves between its buttons.
  - Window lists (`.camp-list`: title, Quick battle, Town): up and down arrows move the focus (`dialog.ts`);
  - A new key or button goes into `keyAction`, into the `keyHelp` list (help screen) and as a `hint()` in the HUD. No keyboard `if` in the scene or the HUD.
- **Text the player sees:** `t('pt', 'en')` from `i18n.ts`; names via `unitName`/`abilityName`. An action's result also as text (`eventLog`, in the help line), not just the floating number.
- **DOM through the pieces of `icons.ts`** (`el`, `icon`, `button` for every text button, `iconButton`) and `dialog.ts`. Inline `style` only for positions and sizes computed at run time. Color is never the only signal.
- **Performance counter** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): fps, frame time and Three resources (`BattleScene.stats`), plus JS memory when the browser reports it. On by default, F3 toggles it (`dt:fps`), text updated 2 times per second.
- **Developer menu** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): menu (H) → Developer. Chooses the map (Hill, Skirmish, Crypt and Gariland's terrain), builds heroes and enemies (class, name, life, speed, movement, jump and abilities), saves to `dt:dev`, exports and imports JSON and opens the battle at `?scenario=dev`. Pure rules in `dev-roster.ts` (`buildDevScenario` creates sheets `dev-h0`, `dev-e0`… with the class's character; `parseSetup` is the JSON boundary), screen in `dev-menu.ts`. - **Town and expedition** (Nov 15, Fernando: the game opens in the town, no world map; the expedition is the F2 of the dungeon loop; `game/crawl.ts`): the title's New game (a new save slot, see Save slots) and Continue (only with a saved game: the list of them; a loaded game with a room in play reopens it, see below) lead to the town (Fernando, Nov 15): every way home goes through `?home` (`goHome`), which boots the walkable town (see below) and then `homeScreen` over it. The gate (`questGate`) and the town menu (`townMenu`) share the quest's rows (`questRows`): with an expedition in progress (`liveExpedition`: `dt:crawl` saved and its boss still up) "Continue the expedition" (floor N of 3) comes first and opens its map, and "Go on the quest" asks a 2nd tap, since it abandons that one; the town menu adds Shop (`openShop`), Tavern (`openBar`, recruits only), Party (`openParty`, every ability) and Title screen (the bare address); the windows are in `town-ui.ts`. The quest takes the town's deployed party (class, name, equipment; max life = the battle sheet's) into `startExpedition`. The expedition map (`expeditionScreen`) shows the floor in play as rows of rooms with each group's enemies (`roomGroup`), the party's life, and Back to town (2nd tap: the descent is lost). A room opens by `?room=<node>`: `selectBattle` refuses a room that is not open (cleared, off the path, typed) and goes home, builds it from the node's seed, depth and boss through `generateRoom`, and `castRoom` puts the party on its hero spawns as dev-roster sheets (`buildDevScenario` under the room's id, so the mood still reads the theme); `newBattle(scenario, room)` sets each hero's carried life, Restart included. Save `dt:battle:expedition`. The room is saved from its first frame (`main.ts` boot). At the end `settleRoom` writes the expedition before any window (`winRoom`, or the save cleared on a defeat or the last boss; a room that is no longer open, ended in another tab, is ignored) and `finishRoom` shows the result (the victory's yellow title on a win; "Floor N cleared!" when the room leads down): back to the map through `?home=expedition` (the only address that opens the map), or to the town. Restart is a free retry with the life the party entered with; leaving the room before it ends loses the expedition (Fernando, Nov 15): Options → Leave the room (2nd tap) or `?home` goes home, where `leftRoom` finds the room still saved, clears both saves and says so, and `?room=` of another room while one is saved goes home the same way. Closing the tab is not leaving: loading the game from the title's Continue finds the room still saved (`savedRoom`, by `roomId`) and reopens it at `?room=<node>`; a room in play travels with its save slot. So Continue has one meaning per screen: the title's lists the saved games and the one loaded resumes (the room in play, else the town), and the town's opens the expedition map. Options and the end-of-battle window name the way home with `homeLabel` (always Town).

Enter or A acts on the prompt (`target`: whoever stands in front, else the door the walker is at, else whoever is beside); walking into a house from its door goes in. The places (`TOWN_PLACES`): the Guild opens Party, the Shop, the Tavern, the dungeon gate (`questGate`), and the Blacksmith and Enchanter a coming-soon window (equipment and gold are another session's work, Nov 15). Four townsfolk (`TOWNSFOLK`) stroll near home and tell their rumours in turn through `talk()` with their painted faces (cosmetic, so `Math.random` there, outside the battle rules). Esc, H, Start, the right button or the corner button open the town menu. Nothing new is saved: everyone starts at the same place on every visit.

- **Living water** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "prettier water… transparent… with water physics"): `water.ts` draws the surface (one `InstancedMesh`, Phong with `onBeforeCompile`: shallow clear and transparent, deep dark, foam at the shore, a glint that runs) over the gravel bed, which sits where `stand()` puts the feet (pure, tested heights in `water-levels.ts`). The water reacts to the characters by itself, through their position every frame (`Water.update`, called every frame through `Board.update`): entering splashes by the speed on the ground (jump ~2.2 m/s, push ~3.8: the push splashes more; sync and undo, which place the character in one frame, do not splash), walking leaves a wake, standing still emits rings and gets a foam collar. Since the night of Nov 13 (Fernando's request: "truly volumetric water, with physics") it is real physics: `WaveField` (pure, tested in `tests/water-field.test.ts`) is an 8x8-cell-per-tile height field with a damped wave equation on the CPU, in fixed steps of 1/60 s of the battle clock; the wave reflects off the shore and the step, the river current (`River` in `terrain.ts`) drags the field and the drawing, and the field sleeps when it calms. The field goes up to the GPU in an 8-bit texture and displaces the vertices of a single subdivided mesh (8x8 per tile; neighboring tiles at the same height become one continuous surface). Color in 3 bands by depth (shallow transparent, deep dark); a water step becomes an arched waterfall and the map edge shows the cut of the volume. Touch is an invisible plane per tile (`pickTiles`). In the pixel art normals pass the mesh stays flat with an up normal (the displacement and the wave lighting exist only in the color shader), so the waves get no outline. The water comes before the other transparents (`renderOrder -1`), so marks, path, cursor and team ring stay on top.

A mood may darken the scene's sky and sun and change the sun's position (`MOODS.*.light`, today only Ash Wood: low light from the side), which changes the board's light too; it applies on top of the map's light. The scene only calls `loadAmbience`/`mount`, `update`, `setDaylight` and `dispose`.
- **Game feel** (Fernando's request, Nov 12: "more responsive and enjoyable to play"; on Nov 13 he asked for the opposite on pacing: "slow walking… a really realistic jump… I don't want fast animations", and the `timing.ts` timings got ~40% slower):
  - Walking at a step (`Walking_A`, 0.6 s per tile) and every step up or down is a jump in 3 parts (`Units.jump` in `scene/units.ts`: crouch with `Jump_Start`, fly in an arc with `Jump_Idle`, land with `Jump_Land`); the duration of each step comes from `stepMs` (`timing.ts`), which the footstep sound also uses (on a jump, the sound goes on landing). Clips in `animations.glb` (`scripts/assets/anim-pack.mjs`).
  - Every wait and animation in battle uses `BattleScene.wait` or `Timeline.animate`, which run on the battle clock (`Timeline` in `scene/timeline.ts`). Holding Enter, Space or A speeds up 3 times; on touch, the Fast battle row of the corner menu toggles it (`dt:fast`) (`pace`/`speed()` in `timing.ts`). No `setTimeout` for battle timing.
  - Strike with a pause on impact (`hitStop`), target flashing white and shaking (`shake`). Heal flashes green, a miss shows a little step back, whoever dies blinks 3 times (`blink`), marked tiles breathe. A strike of 25% of life or more shakes the camera and enlarges the number; whoever starts the turn does a little hop (`pop`); the cursor slides between tiles. Each character has its own copy of the materials.
  - Confirm only on press (no key repeat).
  - The camera opens close (`PC_START`) and frames the attacker and the target.
  - Action camera on the strike (`CameraRig.actionCam` in `scene/camera.ts`, Nov 13, Fernando's request: "XCOM-style action cam" and then "try several options such as over the shoulder, from behind the enemy"): `ACTION_CAMS` there holds the options and `?camera=<name>` picks one to compare (`hero`, the default since Fernando's choice on Nov 13: `shoulder` when the hero attacks and `behind` when it is the enemy, always from the heroes' side; `xcom`, the previous one; `shoulder`; `behind`; `side`; `top`; `none`). The low-camera ones change the tilt (`pitch`), aim at character height and cut what is in front with the camera's clipping plane (`clip`), because the outer woods and the cliffs covered the strike. Zoom applies through `frameView()` every frame (before, the action camera changed `viewH` and nothing happened).
  - The turn list shows every unit (Nov 15, Fernando's decision; the short list of 4 and its L key are gone), scrolling within the column above the acting panel: a new turn scrolls back to NOW, a redraw keeps where the player scrolled, and the charge row of "Execute?" is kept in view.
  - Menus open in 90–120 ms and the ▶ pulses; with reduced motion, nothing animates.
- **Terrain that does not look like Minecraft** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "more diagonal tiles… it doesn't need to be all straight" and "bigger trees… some can take up more than 1 tile"), all just drawing in `scene/board.ts` (the climb in `scene/space.ts`):
  - **Ramp** (`Tile.ramp`, data in `src/content/maps/`): `slope`/`surfaceY` (`TileSpace` in `scene/space.ts`) are the single calculation of the climb; the tile top, the character (`world`), the marks, the cursor (`lay`) and the character's ring and arrow (`tiltGround`) lie on it. Walking to/from a ramp follows the ground (`walkY`) at the flat step's pace; going up from the ramp to the tile above is still a jump (the rule measures by standing height, and the footstep sound counts on that). Dungeon floor (`LOOK.stairs`) draws the ramp as stairs (`buildStairs`), sunk 1 step so nothing disappears behind the steps.
  - **Trees** (`buildTrees`): bigger than the tile (`TREE_SIZE`); neighboring forest tiles at the same height become one tree (2x2, then 2x1). The canopy has a cutout (`cutMaterial`): the part in front of a character, the cursor or a marked tile disappears, with a checkerboard edge. The tree does not accept the pixel art normals material (`allowOverride = false`) and returns its own normal in the normals pass, otherwise the outline of the cut canopy showed up over the character.
  - **Painted pieces** (Nov 13, Fernando put this front on "model the maps and objects" in the painted style he chose): on the open maps, trees (`trees.ts`) and objects (`props.ts`: crates, barrels, rocks, bottles, sacks, bridge debris and the remains beside corpses) are assembled by code and take the place of the KayKit pieces by the same name (`swapTrees`, `swapProps`); the crypts stay with KayKit.

    - Bridge (`bridge.ts`): where the bank is at the plank height they extend 1/4 of a tile over the earth (the terrain rounds off the edge); a bridge of 3 tiles or more gets a middle beam and a broken railing; a bridge tile with `rubble` terrain gets the debris on top.
  - **Tile marks** (Nov 13, Fernando found the flat white square "too white"): thin frame in the color of the type with a dark line outside, weak center and one pattern per type (`MARK` and `markTexture` in `scene/marks.ts`): plain blue for moving, orange stripes for the strike range, filled for the chosen tile and the area, purple X for the blocked shot, red X where the square of a unit bigger than one cell does not fit (a 1-cell unit keeps the plain cursor, Nov 14 review). The aim mark (Nov 15, Fernando: no 2x2 preview on empty ground) is one half-tile cell, or the whole body of the unit the strike lands on, the Colossus' 4x4 included (`aimMark` in `interaction.ts`: the rule aims at the whole tile, so a token on its corner is the one hit even from an empty cell beside it); the cursor frames the same square, and a keyboard step that would leave the mark where it was goes on until it moves, so one press crosses a body. An area aimed at empty ground keeps that one cell and marks the rest of the area the rule hits, centred on the aimed whole tile. Canvas texture without mipmaps; the color comes from the material. The help text says "marked tile", without naming the color.

- **Save slots** (Nov 15, Fernando: "save games so New game does not erase the progress"): the game reads and writes its progress keys as before; a slot is a copy of them (`PROGRESS_KEYS` plus `dt:played`) in the `dt:saves` manifest (`saves.ts`, pure and tested; `parseManifest` is the trust boundary). The active slot's truth is the live keys: `readSaves` writes them into it, and live progress with no slot (the single save from before, a town reached by `?home`) becomes a new slot. New game, load and delete check every write in the browser itself (`store.saved`, not the tab's memory) and stop at a refused one (full storage): the manifest goes first, with no slot in play while the live keys change, so a refused key puts the game in play back and never leaves a slot holding a mix of two games; deleting the slot in play clears the live keys. Slot ids are safe integers, a new slot takes the first free one, and a stored manifest the parse cannot give back whole is copied to `dt:saves:bad` before it is replaced. `store.set` stamps `dt:played` on every progress write. The window is `game/saves-ui.ts` (Continue's list, the latest played first; Delete asks a 2nd tap).
