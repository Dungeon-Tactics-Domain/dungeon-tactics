# src/client — browser

Applies together with the root `CLAUDE.md`. **Web on PC first** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): adapting for mobile is its own stage later; until then mobile only needs to open without errors (do not delete the media queries or touch input).

- **Content is data in `src/content`** (Nov 14): stages, speakers and lines (`campaign.ts`), the opening, world nodes, towns and rumors (`world.ts`), quests (`quests.ts`) and the story party and recruit names (`party.ts`). The files here keep the types and the rules (`campaign.ts`, `world.ts`, `missions.ts`, `group.ts`) and re-export the data their callers used before.
- **Workflow:** one developer (Fernando) commits straight to `master`, no PRs; there is a single live environment, https://dungeon-tactics.fernandorffdev.com.
- **Who owns what:** `main.ts` (`Game`) wires everything and owns the turn loop and the turn screen; `command.ts` says what the menu shows and what comes next (pure, tested); `interaction.ts` decides what the chosen tile does (pure, tested); `scene.ts` (`BattleScene`) only draws; `hud.ts` (`BattleHud`) only shows and passes clicks along; `touch-input.ts` (`TouchInput`) only translates gestures and mouse; `gamepad.ts` translates the gamepad into keyboard actions (pure, tested). A class holding a resource (WebGL, listeners) has `dispose()`.
- **Interface in the FFT battle-screen style** (Fernando's decision, Nov 12: "discard the current interfaces and recreate the interface the same way as the original final fantasy tactics"). **The interface follows the design system (`docs/game-design/design-system.md`) as the source of truth**; the list below says what is implemented:
  - **Turn screens** (`Screen` in `command.ts`): command menu → abilities submenu → map (choose the tile) → "Execute?" → menu. Waiting leads to the direction choice on maps with direction.
  - **`BattleHud` pieces:**
    - top left corner: the tile under the cursor (`tileInfo`) and the list of upcoming turns with portraits;
    - top middle: the help line and, below it, the ability name banner;
    - beside the acting character (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "beside the character like in FFT"): Move · Act · Wait · Status menu, submenu and the "Execute?" window with hit chance, effect and life before → after. `BattleScene.screenOf` gives the character on screen, `placeBeside` (`hud.ts`, pure and tested) picks the side (right; with no room, left) and slides in height so as not to cover the turn list or the "Execute?" target; `Game.placeMenu` repositions every frame (only left/top, without redoing the DOM), because the camera slides and rotates with the menu open;
    - moved and did not act: the Move row becomes "Undo move" and Esc (B, right button) on the menu undoes the move, for free, like Tactics Ogre (Nov 13, Fernando's request; `canUndoMove` in `command.ts`). After acting, only Z undoes;
    - bottom: panel of the acting unit (left) and of the unit under the cursor (right), with portrait and HP, MP and CT bars. The target panel is mirrored (portrait on the right). When aiming (Act map and "Execute?"), the strike preview comes out in the Tactics Ogre style (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "the attack preview interface should be like this one from Tactics Ogre (Reborn)"; our own design): in the gap between the two panels, a translucent arrow-shaped strip per target (`strikes`, up to 3, the rest in "+N targets"), with "HP", the big damage (−12–15 PTS; on a heal +7–10 in green) and ⊕ hit chance, and below it the `strikeInfo` line (name, ↶ from behind / ⇥ from the side, push and spikes, "life 25 → 13–16", ALLY!); on the target's life bar, the part that goes away in stripes. The calculation is one (`forecast` in `hud.ts`) for the strips and for "Execute?". The strips stay inside the height of the bottom panels and go into `sizes.bottom` (so "Execute?" does not land on top); only the acting unit's panel counts in the height the scene discounts (`insets`), otherwise the map jumped when aiming;
    - top right corner: menu button (3 bars, on the parchment) that opens the list Undo, rotate, view the map, pixel, Fast battle and Options, each row with its key (Nov 13, Fernando's request: the row of 7 stone buttons was "crude and inconsistent"); the performance counter right below.
  - **What is not copied:** art, font, icons, portraits, frames, sounds and text of the original. Parchment frame built in CSS (`--paper*`, `--ink*`, `--focus` in `:root`), portraits of our KayKit characters (`public/chars`, `scripts/portraits.mjs`), and our own ▶ arrow in place of the menu hand.
  - **Windows (`dialog()`) on the same parchment** (Nov 13, Fernando's choice "everything in parchment", previously dark purple stone): title, Options, Status, How to play, Credits, Party, stage end and Developer. Inside them, text in `--ink`/`--ink-muted`, highlight and link in `--focus`, paper buttons with an ink outline (the primary stays gold), stars in `--star-ink`; every color passes 4.5:1 (stars, large text, 3:1).
  - **Design system** (Nov 13, Fernando's request): tokens, pieces with their states, input, measured contrast and debts in `docs/game-design/design-system.md`. Change a token or a piece, change the document in the same commit. Focus outside the parchment takes `--focus-halo`; a disabled button says why in its text.
  - **New HUD piece:** `.panel` (parchment) with `.item` (menu row, `.focus` marks the focus), `gauge()` (bar with label and number) and `unitPanel()` in `hud.ts`. Colors and sizes only through the `:root` variables.
- **Input:** keyboard, mouse and gamepad move the same cursor and go through the same `Game.runKey`.
  - Keyboard: `keyboard.ts` (WASD and arrows go up and down the menu and move the cursor on the map; Enter confirms; Esc goes back; P Wait; C Status; F3 counter; Z undo).
  - Mouse: hovering chooses, click confirms, right button goes back.
  - Gamepad (Gamepad API, standard Xbox and PlayStation mapping): d-pad and analog stick move; A or × confirms; B or ○ goes back; X or □ Status; shoulders rotate the camera; triggers zoom; Back undoes; Start opens the menu. With a dialog open, the gamepad moves between its buttons.
  - A new key or button goes into `keyAction`, into the `keyHelp` list (help screen) and as a `hint()` in the HUD. No keyboard `if` in the scene or the HUD.
- **Text the player sees:** `t('pt', 'en')` from `i18n.ts`; names via `unitName`/`abilityName`. An action's result also as text (`eventLog`, in the help line), not just the floating number.
- **DOM through the pieces of `icons.ts`** (`el`, `icon`, `iconButton`) and `dialog.ts`. Color is never the only signal.
- **Performance counter** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): fps, frame time and Three resources (`BattleScene.stats`), plus JS memory when the browser reports it. On by default, F3 toggles it (`dt:fps`), text updated 2 times per second.
- **Developer menu** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): menu (H) → Developer. Chooses the map (Hill, Skirmish, Crypt and Gariland's terrain), builds heroes and enemies (class, name, life, speed, movement, jump and abilities), saves to `dt:dev`, exports and imports JSON and opens the battle at `?scenario=dev`. Pure rules in `dev-roster.ts` (`buildDevScenario` creates sheets `dev-h0`, `dev-e0`… with the class's character; `parseSetup` is the JSON boundary), screen in `dev-menu.ts`. Units occupy the map's start tiles; with no Beacon on the map, whoever is left wins.
- **Fixed title screen** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "on the title, pressing Esc starts a battle… I want a real title screen"): `dialog(…, { fixed: true })` does not close; the screens that leave it (Standalone battle, How to play, Credits, Developer) go back with Esc/✕ via `onBack`. With any window open, `#app` is `inert` (`dialog.ts`). The Hill in the background is just scenery.
- **Game from start to finish** (night of Nov 12): title (`titleScreen`: Continue, New game, Standalone battle, Developer) → opening (`INTRO` in `src/content/world.ts`, `talk` with `dark`) → protagonist creation (`create-ui.ts`, rules in `hero.ts`, saved in `dt:hero`) → world map (`?world`, pure `world.ts` and `world-ui.ts`, progress in `dt:world`) → stages (`?stage=<n>`) → back to the map. The protagonist takes the place of the companion of the same class (`withHero`) and speaks as `hero` in the conversations.
- **"Beacon Ashes" campaign** (Fernando's request, Nov 12): stages, casts and lines in `src/content/campaign.ts`, rules in `campaign.ts` (pure, tested; each stage is a cast from `dev-roster.ts`, scenario `camp<n>`, opened by `?stage=<n>` only after beating the previous one, progress in `dt:campaign`). The dialogue box is in `talk.ts`, with letter-by-letter text and a blip per voice (`blip` in `sound.ts`); Esc skips the conversation. Two acts (5 + 2 stages), music per stage (`song`, a CC0 .ogg file from `public/music/` with per-track gain in `music.ts`, played in a loop with a 0.5 s fade by `sound.ts`; Nov 13, Fernando's request: "use free music instead of the ones you created"), fixed gold per victory (`VICTORY_GOLD`; the end-of-stage stars were removed on Nov 13, Fernando's request: no puzzle feel), abilities unlocked little by little (`party(n)`: 2, 3 and 4; `learned(n)` announces at the end of the stage). With no URL parameter the game opens the title screen (`titleScreen` in `main.ts`); victory drops confetti (`confetti`). Difficulty is measured with `MEASURE=300 npx vitest run tests/campaign.test.ts --silent=false`: the heroes' win rate must fall stage by stage.
- **Party, recruiting and class change** (Nov 13, delivery 5 of `docs/game-design/towns-party-equipment.md`): pure rules in `group.ts` (`dt:party`): the 4 from the story never leave, recruits join through the towns' Bar (`RECRUIT_COST`, cap `GROUP_MAX` 8) and are dismissed on the sheet, anyone switches among the classes of `JOBS` (the 4 starting ones and the new ones: Lancer since Nov 13, with the barbarian's character until it gets its own), up to 4 go to battle (`deployed`). `heroesFor` builds who fights (stages, missions and the learned-abilities notice); the default party gives exactly the measured cast of each stage. Equipment is kept by member id (the story ones use the starting class, like the old save). Screens in `world-ui.ts` (`openParty`, `openSheet`, `openBar`).
- **Battle highlight** (night of Nov 12): `tally.ts` (pure, tested) sums damage and downed units of each unit from the events `Game.animate` receives; the end screen shows the hero who downed the most (tie broken by damage). Memory only: reopening mid-battle resets it.
- **Until Dawn mode** (night of Nov 12): in the Standalone battle, `?scenario=survival` assembles in `survival.ts` the 4 heroes against enemies with 2x life that are born on the heroes' side of the river, in Ash Wood (ambush: when born on the other side, just passing the turn won 100%); wins on reaching `SURVIVE_TURNS` turns (all the turns, the same the list counts: its title shows how many are left) or by downing everyone. Measurement: `MEASURE=300 npx vitest run tests/survival.test.ts --silent=false`.
- **Living water** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "prettier water… transparent… with water physics"): `water.ts` draws the surface (one `InstancedMesh`, Phong with `onBeforeCompile`: shallow clear and transparent, deep dark, foam at the shore, a glint that runs) over the gravel bed, which sits where `stand()` puts the feet (pure, tested heights in `water-levels.ts`). The water reacts to the characters by itself, through their position every frame (`Water.update` in `frame`): entering splashes by the speed on the ground (jump ~2.2 m/s, push ~3.8: the push splashes more; sync and undo, which place the character in one frame, do not splash), walking leaves a wake, standing still emits rings and gets a foam collar. Since the night of Nov 13 (Fernando's request: "truly volumetric water, with physics") it is real physics: `WaveField` (pure, tested in `tests/water-field.test.ts`) is an 8x8-cell-per-tile height field with a damped wave equation on the CPU, in fixed steps of 1/60 s of the battle clock; the wave reflects off the shore and the step, the river current (`River` in `terrain.ts`) drags the field and the drawing, and the field sleeps when it calms. The field goes up to the GPU in an 8-bit texture and displaces the vertices of a single subdivided mesh (8x8 per tile; neighboring tiles at the same height become one continuous surface). Color in 3 bands by depth (shallow transparent, deep dark); a water step becomes an arched waterfall and the map edge shows the cut of the volume. Touch is an invisible plane per tile (`pickTiles`). In the pixel art normals pass the mesh stays flat with an up normal (the displacement and the wave lighting exist only in the color shader), so the waves get no outline. The water comes before the other transparents (`renderOrder -1`), so marks, path, cursor and team ring stay on top.
- **Flow timings** in `timing.ts`; gesture and effect of each ability in `fx.ts`; sound in `sound.ts`.
- **Board surroundings** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "more realistic scenery… fog, lighting, godrays"): `ambience.ts`, one mood per map (`MOODS`: forest, field, crypt, snow; the map comes from the `Grid`). Ground that continues past the edge, models in rings, fog by distance from the edge (only on the surroundings' materials: the board stays sharp), low mist, light rays, torches and particles, on the battle clock. Nothing goes past `LIMIT` (it covers no tile or character at any rotation). A transparent effect does not draw in the pixel art normals pass (`skipNormalPass`). A mood may darken the scene's sky and sun and change the sun's position (`MOODS.*.light`, today only Ash Wood: low light from the side), which changes the board's light too; it applies on top of the stage's light. The scene only calls `loadAmbience`/`mount`, `update`, `setDaylight` and `dispose`.
- **Game feel** (Fernando's request, Nov 12: "more responsive and enjoyable to play"; on Nov 13 he asked for the opposite on pacing: "slow walking… a really realistic jump… I don't want fast animations", and the `timing.ts` timings got ~40% slower):
  - Walking at a step (`Walking_A`, 0.6 s per tile) and every step up or down is a jump in 3 parts (`BattleScene.jump`: crouch with `Jump_Start`, fly in an arc with `Jump_Idle`, land with `Jump_Land`); the duration of each step comes from `stepMs` (`timing.ts`), which the footstep sound also uses (on a jump, the sound goes on landing). Clips in `animations.glb` (`scripts/assets/anim-pack.mjs`).
  - Every wait and animation in battle uses `BattleScene.wait`/`animate`, which run on the battle clock (`Timeline` in `scene/timeline.ts`). Holding Enter, Space or A speeds up 3 times; on touch, the Fast battle row of the corner menu toggles it (`dt:fast`) (`pace`/`speed()` in `timing.ts`). No `setTimeout` for battle timing.
  - Strike with a pause on impact (`hitStop`), target flashing white and shaking (`shake`). Heal flashes green, a miss shows a little step back, whoever dies blinks 3 times (`blink`), marked tiles breathe. A strike of 25% of life or more shakes the camera and enlarges the number; whoever starts the turn does a little hop (`pop`); the cursor slides between tiles. Each character has its own copy of the materials.
  - Confirm only on press (no key repeat).
  - The camera opens close (`PC_START`) and frames the attacker and the target.
  - Action camera on the strike (`CameraRig.actionCam` in `scene/camera.ts`, Nov 13, Fernando's request: "XCOM-style action cam" and then "try several options such as over the shoulder, from behind the enemy"): `ACTION_CAMS` there holds the options and `?camera=<name>` picks one to compare (`hero`, the default since Fernando's choice on Nov 13: `shoulder` when the hero attacks and `behind` when it is the enemy, always from the heroes' side; `xcom`, the previous one; `shoulder`; `behind`; `side`; `top`; `none`). The low-camera ones change the tilt (`pitch`), aim at character height and cut what is in front with the camera's clipping plane (`clip`), because the outer woods and the cliffs covered the strike. Zoom applies through `frameView()` every frame (before, the action camera changed `viewH` and nothing happened).
  - Short turn list (4) by default, L shows everything (`dt:turns`).
  - Menus open in 90–120 ms and the ▶ pulses; with reduced motion, nothing animates.
- **Terrain that does not look like Minecraft** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "more diagonal tiles… it doesn't need to be all straight" and "bigger trees… some can take up more than 1 tile"), all just drawing in `scene/board.ts` (the climb in `scene/space.ts`):
  - **Ramp** (`Tile.ramp`, data in `src/content/maps/`): `slope`/`surfaceY` (`TileSpace` in `scene/space.ts`) are the single calculation of the climb; the tile top, the character (`world`), the marks, the cursor (`lay`) and the character's ring and arrow (`tiltGround`) lie on it. Walking to/from a ramp follows the ground (`walkY`) at the flat step's pace; going up from the ramp to the tile above is still a jump (the rule measures by standing height, and the footstep sound counts on that). Dungeon floor (`LOOK.stairs`) draws the ramp as stairs (`buildStairs`), sunk 1 step so nothing disappears behind the steps.
  - **Trees** (`buildTrees`): bigger than the tile (`TREE_SIZE`); neighboring forest tiles at the same height become one tree (2x2, then 2x1). The canopy has a cutout (`cutMaterial`): the part in front of a character, the cursor or a marked tile disappears, with a checkerboard edge. The tree does not accept the pixel art normals material (`allowOverride = false`) and returns its own normal in the normals pass, otherwise the outline of the cut canopy showed up over the character.
  - **Painted pieces** (Nov 13, Fernando put this front on "model the maps and objects" in the painted style he chose): on the open maps, trees (`trees.ts`) and objects (`props.ts`: crates, barrels, rocks, bottles, sacks, bridge debris and the remains beside corpses) are assembled by code and take the place of the KayKit pieces by the same name (`swapTrees`, `swapProps`); the crypts stay with KayKit.
    - A single material (`painted.ts`): paint color per vertex in tone masses (`ramp`, with a short step) and the neutral `tex/paint.jpg` ink on top; one mesh per piece. Base pieces: `tube` (trunk, branch, root, barrel), `lump` (canopy tuft, rock, sack), `slab` (plank).
    - Convention: y = 0 is the ground and what goes below (trunk skirt, root) enters the earth; `userData.box` is the box above ground, the one that counts for measuring (`boxOf`), `userData.foot` the foot radius (the scene does not let it go past the flat core of the tile, 0.4) and `userData.tall` lets the pine go past the `TREE_SIZE` ceiling.
    - Variations: `name`, `name~1`, `name~2`… in the piece map; `variant(pieces, name, draw)` picks by position. The surroundings use the light version (`swapTrees(models, true)`), otherwise the outer woods cost twice the triangles of the rest of the scene.
    - Characters and corpses no longer carry the grain from `detail.ts`, only light shading (`Board.shaded`); the grain stayed for the remaining KayKit pieces (brick, Gariland's crate).
    - Bridge (`bridge.ts`): where the bank is at the plank height they extend 1/4 of a tile over the earth (the terrain rounds off the edge); a bridge of 3 tiles or more gets a middle beam and a broken railing; a bridge tile with `rubble` terrain gets the debris on top.
  - **Tile marks** (Nov 13, Fernando found the flat white square "too white"): thin frame in the color of the type with a dark line outside, weak center and one pattern per type (`MARK` and `markTexture` in `scene/marks.ts`): plain blue for moving, orange stripes for the strike range, filled for the chosen tile and the area, purple X for the blocked shot. Canvas texture without mipmaps; the color comes from the material. The help text says "marked tile", without naming the color.
  - **Details** (`buildDetails`): stones at the foot of cliffs and tufts or pebbles in the corner of some tiles, outside the character's ring (0.44). Fixed draw by position (`hash`).
  - **Cost:** a column cube that never shows is not drawn (`buried`: its 4 neighbors of solid ground reach its top; water and bridge are left out). On the Hill the triangles dropped from 1.2 million to 0.73 million, which pays for the trees and details. A different tone per tile on top (per-instance color) was measured and rejected: it cost 16% to 28% of the frames in Chrome without a graphics card, barely visible.
- **Configurable pixel art** (Fernando's request, Oct 25): `pixel-settings.ts` (pure) keeps size, outline, palette and locked camera in `dt:pixel`; `BattleScene.setPixel` applies it at once. Shortcut V and the Pixel size row in the corner menu; options in the Video and effects section of Options with `choice()` (`dialog.ts`).
- **Wide Options window with sections** (Nov 13, Fernando's request: "the menu could be something that takes up more of the screen width, with subsections"): `Game.menu` in `main.ts`, up to 1400 px wide, sections Game (objective, legend, text speed), Sound, Video and effects, Controls and Language in a left column (a vertical `choice()`) and one section at a time on the right; Restart, Map, Title and Developer at the foot. The gamepad only moves through what is in view (`pollPad` filters the hidden section).
- **Volume as a bar in 5% steps** (Nov 13, Fernando's request): `slider()` in `dialog.ts` (native range input, number in % alongside, applies and saves on every movement). Keyboard arrows change the bar by themselves; on the gamepad, left and right on the focused bar change the value (`pollPad`). `dt:sound` keeps `{ music, sfx }` from 0 to 100; the old save (0, 1, 2) becomes 0, 45 and 100 in `parseSound`, and 100% is the old "high" (gain 0.8).
- Preferences and saves in `store.ts` with the `dt:` prefix. A change of key or format goes into `MIGRATIONS` (`store.ts`, runs once per browser on load, counted in `dt:migrated`), so nobody loses progress.
- **Testing without music** (Fernando's request, Nov 13): on `localhost` the music starts at 0 and, on a page opened by automation (`navigator.webdriver`, Playwright), it never plays unless the URL has `?music=1`. Test scripts launch Chrome with `--mute-audio`.
